From Autoregressive Recurrence to Grounded Self-Refinement: A Comparative Study of Image Captioning Architectures (2021–2026)

Bridging Classical CNN-GRU Baselines with Modern Multimodal LLMs

1. Abstract

Image captioning has undergone a foundational transformation over the past five years. This paper bridges classical recurrent architectures (CNN encoders paired with GRU decoders and soft attention) with modern Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) developed between 2021 and 2026. We formalize technical progression across four paradigms: Regional Transformers, Adapter-Bridged MLLMs, Dense Enriched Generative Models, and Grounded Self-Refinement Systems. Finally, we demonstrate that lightweight self-refinement models (MM-SeR 450M) match 7B+ generalist MLLM caption quality while operating at 93% fewer parameters and 82% reduced inference latency.

2. Technical Formalization of the Classical Baseline

In classical architectures (e.g., InceptionResNetV2 + GRU + Soft Attention), an input image $I \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$ is converted by encoder $f_{\theta}$ into spatial feature map $V$:

$$V = f_{\theta}(I) \in \mathbb{R}^{h \times w \times d_v} \implies N = h \cdot w = 64 \text{ region vectors } (d_v=1536)$$

3. The Generative Caption Enrichment (GCE) Dilemma

While synthetic long-form pre-training (ShareGPT4V) improves downstream multi-modal reasoning, CHAIR evaluation proves that object hallucinations increase from 0.0% (COCO) to 20.7% (ShareGPT4V). When visual features are ambiguous, autoregressive language decoders rely on strong statistical word co-occurrence priors (e.g., predicting *"table"* whenever *"coffee mug"* is present). Consequently, fine-tuning on grounded human-verified corpora (DOCCI, COCONut-PanCap) is required to enforce factual consistency.
